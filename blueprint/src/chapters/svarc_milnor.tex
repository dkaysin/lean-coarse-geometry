\chapter{The \v{S}varc--Milnor lemma}
\label{chap:svarc-milnor}

Milestone M4. A group acting isometrically, metrically properly and coboundedly on a coarsely
geodesic space is finitely generated, and its orbit maps are quasi-isometries for the word metric
of any finite generating family (L\"oh, Prop.~5.4.1). The chapter has the hypotheses on the
action, the lemma, its topological form (Corollary 5.4.2), and corollaries for groups acting on
groups and on $\R^n$.

\section{The lemma}

Let a group $G$ act by isometries on a pseudometric space $X$. Assume that $X$ is
\emph{coarsely geodesic} (Chapter~\ref{chap:coarsely-geodesic}): there are constants $r, L, C$
such that any two points $x, y$ are joined by a chain $x = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n = y$ with
$d(x_i, x_{i+1}) \le r$ and $n \le L\,d(x, y) + C$. Assume that the action is \emph{cobounded}:
some bounded $s \subseteq X$ has translates covering $X$. Fix $x_0 \in s$, let $B$ be the closed
$r$-thickening of $s$, which is bounded, and put
\[ S = \{g \in G \mid g B \cap B \ne \emptyset\}. \]
Then
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $S$ generates $G$;
  \item $d(g x_0, h x_0) \le 2 \diam B\,|g^{-1}h|_S$ and
        $|g^{-1}h|_S \le L\,d(g x_0, h x_0) + C + 1$ for all $g, h \in G$;
  \item every point of $X$ is within $\diam s$ of the orbit $G x_0$;
  \item if moreover the action is \emph{metrically proper}, then $S$ is finite, so $G$ is
        finitely generated, and the orbit map is a quasi-isometry for the word metric of any
        finite generating family.
\end{enumerate}
Parts 1--3 need no properness. Part 1 uses only the existence of $r$-chains; the bound on their
length is needed for the lower bound in part 2 and cannot be dropped: $\Z$ acting by
translations on $(\R, \sqrt{d})$ satisfies every other hypothesis, but its orbit map is not a
quasi-isometry.

In the nodes below $X$ is nonempty. Both proofs use the set $S$, the set in the definition of a
metrically proper action, written in Lean with Mathlib's
\texttt{Metric.cthickening} and no new definition; it is L\"oh's generating set in Prop.~5.4.1.
The steps are stated with the word length and no metric on $G$ (design decision D8). The
hypotheses give a bounded $s$ whose translates cover $X$ but need not contain $x_0$; both proofs
replace $s$ by $s \cup \{x_0\}$, which is still bounded. Both proofs choose, for each $y \in X$, a
translate $\psi(y) s$ containing $y$; the map $\psi \colon X \to G$ is a quasi-inverse of the orbit
map, and $r$-close points have values of $\psi$ that differ by an element of $S$.

\section{Topological form}

L\"oh states the lemma also with topological hypotheses (Corollary 5.4.2): a proper geodesic
space, and an action that is proper (for every compact $K$, only finitely many $g$ have $g K \cap
K \ne \emptyset$; Mathlib's \texttt{ProperlyDiscontinuousSMul}) and cocompact (the orbit space is
compact). These imply the hypotheses above
(Lemmas~\ref{isMetricallyProperSMul_of_properlyDiscontinuousSMul},
\ref{isCoboundedSMul_of_compactSpace_quotient} and
\ref{Metric.IsGeodesicSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}).
