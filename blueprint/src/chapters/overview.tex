\chapter{Overview}
\label{chap:overview}

This blueprint formalizes parts of coarse geometry and geometric group theory in Lean~4, in a
form that can move into Mathlib. Work is organized by \emph{targets}: definitions and theorems
chosen one at a time. For each target the blueprint contains its statement and the definitions
that Mathlib does not have yet and the statement needs, then the results its proof needs.
Definitions and statements are formalized first, with the proofs left open, and proved later;
so a node may have its statement formalized long before its proof.

Each target follows a reference text. The default is C.~L\"oh, \emph{Geometric Group Theory: An
Introduction}, Springer, 2017; item numbers without a book refer to it. Hyperbolic spaces also
follow M.~R.~Bridson and A.~Haefliger, \emph{Metric Spaces of Non-Positive Curvature},
Springer, 1999. Coarse spaces of bounded geometry follow J.~Roe, \emph{Lectures on
Coarse Geometry}, American Mathematical Society, 2003.

\section*{Targets}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The \v{S}varc--Milnor lemma (Chapter~\ref{chap:svarc-milnor}) and its corollaries:
        independence of the word metric from the finite generating family up to
        quasi-isometry, finite-index subgroups, $\Z^n \sim_{QI} \R^n$
        (Chapter~\ref{chap:corollaries}).
  \item The definitions of hyperbolicity (Gromov's four-point condition, slim geodesic triangles,
        quasi-hyperbolicity) and the equivalences between them, among them L\"oh's definition of
        hyperbolic groups and the independence of hyperbolicity of a group from its generating
        family (Chapter~\ref{chap:hyperbolic}).
  \item Uniformly discrete spaces and spaces of bounded geometry, for metric spaces and for
        coarse spaces, and their comparison on metric spaces
        (Chapter~\ref{chap:bounded-geometry}).
\end{itemize}

\section*{The \v{S}varc--Milnor lemma}

Let a group $G$ act by isometries on a pseudometric space $X$. Assume that $X$ is
\emph{coarsely geodesic}: there are constants $r, L, C$ such that any two points $x, y$ are joined
by a chain $x = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n = y$ with $d(x_i, x_{i+1}) \le r$ and $n \le L\,d(x, y) + C$.
Assume that the action is \emph{cobounded}: some bounded $s \subseteq X$ has translates covering
$X$. Fix $x_0 \in s$, let $B$ be the closed $r$-thickening of $s$, which is bounded, and put
$S = \{g \in G \mid g B \cap B \ne \emptyset\}$. Then
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $S$ generates $G$;
  \item $d(g x_0, h x_0) \le 2 \diam B\,|g^{-1}h|_S$ and
        $|g^{-1}h|_S \le L\,d(g x_0, h x_0) + C + 1$ for all $g, h \in G$;
  \item every point of $X$ is within $\diam s$ of the orbit $G x_0$;
  \item if moreover the action is \emph{metrically proper}, then $S$ is finite, so $G$ is
        finitely generated, and the orbit map is a quasi-isometry for the word metric of any
        finite generating family.
\end{enumerate}
Parts 1--3 need no properness. Part 1 uses only the existence of $r$-chains; the bound on their
length is needed for the lower bound in part 2 and cannot be dropped: $\Z$ acting by
translations on $(\R, \sqrt{d})$ satisfies every other hypothesis, but its orbit map is not a
quasi-isometry.

\subsection*{Why chains}

The chain hypothesis replaces quasi-geodesic paths. It makes the proof a finite counting
argument, needs no theory of geodesic spaces, and holds for word metrics themselves ($r = L = 1$,
$C = 0$), so the corollaries follow by letting a group act on itself. No generality is lost: in
every pseudometric space the chain condition is equivalent, with other constants, to L\"oh's
(non-continuous) quasi-geodesic condition (Definition 5.3.4).

\section*{Layout}

Chapter~\ref{chap:quasi-isometry} develops quasi-isometries,
Chapter~\ref{chap:word-metric} word metrics, Chapter~\ref{chap:coarsely-geodesic} coarsely
geodesic spaces and Chapter~\ref{chap:actions} the hypotheses on the action.
Chapter~\ref{chap:svarc-milnor} proves the lemma and Chapter~\ref{chap:corollaries} its
corollaries. Chapter~\ref{chap:hyperbolic} collects the definitions of hyperbolicity and the
equivalences between them, and Chapter~\ref{chap:stability} provides the tools some of them need:
the geometric realisation of graphs and the stability of quasi-geodesics.
Chapter~\ref{chap:coarse-spaces} records the coarse-space library the project contains and its
relation to the metric notions.
Chapter~\ref{chap:bounded-geometry} defines uniformly discrete spaces and bounded geometry, for
metric and for coarse spaces.
