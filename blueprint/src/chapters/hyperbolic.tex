\chapter{Hyperbolic spaces and groups}
\label{chap:hyperbolic}

Milestone M7. The definitions of hyperbolic spaces and groups below are copies of open Mathlib
pull requests. They use Gromov's four-point condition, which makes sense in every pseudometric
space. L\"oh (Definition 7.2.2) and Bridson--Haefliger (Definition III.H.1.1) define hyperbolicity
of a geodesic space by slim geodesic triangles instead. This chapter adds that definition and shows
that the two agree on geodesic spaces (L\"oh, Exercise 7.E.10; Bridson--Haefliger,
Proposition III.H.1.22), as a first step towards the independence of the generating family.

Write $(x \cdot y)_w = \frac12\bigl(d(w, x) + d(w, y) - d(x, y)\bigr)$ for the Gromov product
(\texttt{Metric.gromovProduct w x y} in Mathlib; Bridson--Haefliger, Definition III.H.1.19). The
four-point condition with constant $\delta$ says
$(x \cdot z)_w \ge \min\{(x \cdot y)_w, (y \cdot z)_w\} - \delta$ for all $w, x, y, z$; this is
$(\delta)$-hyperbolicity in the sense of Bridson--Haefliger, Definition III.H.1.20.

Mathlib has no geodesics. How to encode them (a map on an interval or its image, as in the
Isabelle library on Gromov hyperbolicity) is an open decision (design decision D17), and so is
the route to Theorem~\ref{thm:hyperbolic-independent} (D18), so the new nodes are not ready.

\begin{definition}[Geodesic space, L\"oh Def.~5.3.1, Bridson--Haefliger Def.~I.1.3]
  \label{def:geodesic}
  \notready
  Let $X$ be a pseudometric space. A \emph{geodesic} from $x$ to $y$ is a map
  $\gamma \colon [0, \ell] \to X$ with $\gamma(0) = x$, $\gamma(\ell) = y$ and
  $d(\gamma(s), \gamma(t)) = |s - t|$ for all $s, t$; then $\ell = d(x, y)$. The space $X$ is
  \emph{geodesic} if any two points are joined by a geodesic.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Slim triangles, L\"oh Def.~7.2.1, Bridson--Haefliger Def.~III.H.1.1]
  \label{def:slim-triangles}
  \notready
  \uses{def:geodesic}
  A \emph{geodesic triangle} with vertices $x, y, z$ in a pseudometric space $X$ consists of
  geodesics from $x$ to $y$, from $y$ to $z$ and from $z$ to $x$, its sides. It is
  \emph{$\delta$-slim} if every point of each side is within distance $\delta$ of a point of one
  of the other two sides. The space $X$ has \emph{$\delta$-slim triangles} if every geodesic
  triangle in $X$ is $\delta$-slim.
\end{definition}

L\"oh and Bridson--Haefliger call a geodesic space with $\delta$-slim triangles
$\delta$-hyperbolic. Here that name belongs to the four-point condition, and the constants of
the two notions differ.

Bridson--Haefliger prove Theorem~\ref{thm:hyperbolic-iff-slim} through thin triangles and the
insize of a triangle (Definition III.H.1.16, Propositions III.H.1.17 and III.H.1.22). The two
lemmas below go directly between slim triangles and the four-point condition instead
(design decision D19), so neither thin triangles nor insize is needed, and the constant is
$3\delta$ in both directions.

\begin{lemma}[Slim triangles give the four-point condition, cf.\ Bridson--Haefliger Prop.~III.H.1.22]
  \label{lem:slim-four-point}
  \notready
  \uses{def:slim-triangles, def:hyperbolic-space}
  If $X$ has $\delta$-slim triangles and is geodesic, then $X$ satisfies the four-point
  condition with constant $3\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  For a geodesic $\sigma$ from $x$ to $y$: (a) $(x \cdot y)_w \le d(w, p)$ for every $p$ on
  $\sigma$, by the triangle inequality; (b) some $p$ on $\sigma$ has
  $d(w, p) \le (x \cdot y)_w + 2\delta$, because by slimness of a triangle $w, x, y$ with side
  $\sigma$ (Definition~\ref{def:slim-triangles}) and connectedness of $\sigma$ some $p$ is within
  $\delta$ of both other sides. Given $w, x, y, z$, take $p$ as in (b) on a side of a triangle
  $x, y, z$. It is within $\delta$ of a point $q$ on another side, say from $x$ to $z$, and (a)
  gives $(x \cdot z)_w \le d(w, q) \le (x \cdot y)_w + 3\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The four-point condition gives slim triangles, cf.\ Bridson--Haefliger Prop.~III.H.1.22]
  \label{lem:four-point-slim}
  \notready
  \uses{def:slim-triangles, def:hyperbolic-space}
  If $X$ satisfies the four-point condition with constant $\delta$, then it has
  $3\delta$-slim triangles.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Let $p$ lie on the side from $x$ to $y$ of a triangle $x, y, z$. As $(x \cdot y)_p = 0$, the
  four-point condition at $p$ gives, say, $(x \cdot z)_p \le \delta$. On the side from $x$ to
  $z$, the intermediate value theorem gives $q$ with $(x \cdot q)_p = (q \cdot z)_p$. Their sum
  is $d(p, q) + (x \cdot z)_p$, and the four-point condition at $p$ bounds each by
  $(x \cdot z)_p + \delta$, so $d(p, q) \le (x \cdot z)_p + 2\delta \le 3\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[L\"oh Ex.~7.E.10, Bridson--Haefliger Prop.~III.H.1.22]
  \label{thm:hyperbolic-iff-slim}
  \notready
  \uses{lem:slim-four-point, lem:four-point-slim}
  A geodesic pseudometric space is hyperbolic if and only if it has $\delta$-slim triangles for
  some $\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  ``If'' is Lemma~\ref{lem:slim-four-point}, ``only if'' is Lemma~\ref{lem:four-point-slim}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Independence of the generating family]
  \label{thm:hyperbolic-independent}
  \notready
  \uses{def:hyperbolic-group, cor:change-of-generators, thm:hyperbolic-iff-slim}
  Whether $G$ with the word metric of a finite generating family is hyperbolic does not depend
  on the family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Not planned yet (design decision D18). Corollary~\ref{cor:change-of-generators} alone does not
  suffice: the four-point condition is not a quasi-isometry invariant of coarsely geodesic spaces
  (the graph of $|x|$ in $\R^2$ is bilipschitz to $\R$ and not hyperbolic). L\"oh defines
  hyperbolic groups by quasi-hyperbolicity of the Cayley graph (slim quasi-geodesic triangles,
  Definition 7.3.1), which is a quasi-isometry invariant directly: quasi-geodesic triangles
  can be pushed forward and pulled back along quasi-isometric embeddings (Proposition 7.2.9).
  For the four-point condition: pass from the word metric to the geometric realisation of the
  Cayley graph, a geodesic space in which it is $\frac12$-dense, with a larger constant; switch
  to slim triangles by Theorem~\ref{thm:hyperbolic-iff-slim}; and carry them along the
  quasi-isometry of Corollary~\ref{cor:change-of-generators}. That last step is L\"oh's
  Corollary 7.2.13 (from Proposition 7.2.9 and Theorem 7.2.10, whose proof uses the stability of
  quasi-geodesics, Theorem 7.2.11), or Bridson--Haefliger Thm.~III.H.1.9 (from
  Thm.~III.H.1.7).
\end{proof}
