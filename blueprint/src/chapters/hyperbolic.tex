\chapter{Definitions of hyperbolicity}
\label{chap:hyperbolic}

Milestone M7. This chapter collects the definitions of hyperbolicity the project uses and the
equivalences between them. There are three, one for each class of spaces on which it is meaningful:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Gromov's \emph{four-point condition}, for every pseudometric space; the definitions of
        hyperbolic spaces and groups below, copies of open Mathlib pull requests, use it;
  \item \emph{slim geodesic triangles}, for geodesic spaces (L\"oh, Definition 7.2.2;
        Bridson--Haefliger, Definition III.H.1.1);
  \item \emph{quasi-hyperbolicity}, slim quasi-geodesic triangles for all constants, for coarsely
        geodesic spaces (L\"oh, Definition 7.2.6).
\end{itemize}
The first two depend on exact distances: an isometry preserves them, a quasi-isometry need not,
even between coarsely geodesic spaces (the graph of $|x|$ in $\R^2$ is bilipschitz to $\R$ and
does not satisfy the four-point condition). Quasi-hyperbolicity is preserved by quasi-isometric
embeddings of coarsely geodesic spaces, by an elementary argument
(Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}).

The equivalences:
\begin{itemize}
  \item on geodesic spaces, the four-point condition and slim triangles agree, by elementary
        arguments (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith});
  \item on geodesic spaces, slim triangles and quasi-hyperbolicity agree
        (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith}); this uses the
        stability of quasi-geodesics (Chapter~\ref{chap:stability});
  \item on word metrics, the four-point condition and quasi-hyperbolicity agree
        (Theorem~\ref{Group.Generators.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff}), through the geometric realisation of
        the Cayley graph (Chapter~\ref{chap:stability}; design decision D28).
\end{itemize}
For groups, the last one shows that the definition of hyperbolic groups below agrees with L\"oh's
(Definition 7.3.1), and that it does not depend on the generating family.

The literature has further definitions, not formalized here: thin triangles and the insize of
triangles (Bridson--Haefliger, Definition III.H.1.16 and Proposition III.H.1.17), the divergence of
geodesics (Bridson--Haefliger, III.H.1.24--1.26), linear isoperimetric inequalities, and, for
groups, linear Dehn functions.

How quasi-hyperbolic spaces are defined is an open design decision (D29); the definition below is
its proposal, and the nodes that use it are not implemented until it is decided.

\section*{The four-point condition}

Write $(x \cdot y)_w = \frac12\bigl(d(w, x) + d(w, y) - d(x, y)\bigr)$ for the Gromov product
(\texttt{Metric.gromovProduct w x y} in Mathlib; Bridson--Haefliger, Definition III.H.1.19). The
four-point condition with constant $\delta$ says
$(x \cdot z)_w \ge \min\{(x \cdot y)_w, (y \cdot z)_w\} - \delta$ for all $w, x, y, z$; this is
$(\delta)$-hyperbolicity in the sense of Bridson--Haefliger, Definition III.H.1.20.

\section*{Quasi-hyperbolic spaces}

Quasi-geodesics are defined in Chapter~\ref{chap:coarsely-geodesic}
(Definition~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiGeodesicWith}).

\begin{definition}[Quasi-hyperbolic space, cf.\ L\"oh Def.~7.2.6]
  \label{Metric.IsQuasiHyperbolic}
  \uses{Metric.IsCoarselyGeodesic, Metric.IsQuasiGeodesicWith}
  A \emph{$(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic triangle} with vertices $x, y, z$ in a pseudometric space $X$
  consists of $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesics from $x$ to $y$, from $y$ to $z$ and from $z$ to $x$, its
  sides. It is \emph{$\delta$-slim} if every point of the image of each side is within distance
  $\delta$ of a point of the image of one of the other two sides. The space $X$ is
  \emph{quasi-hyperbolic} if it is coarsely geodesic
  (Definition~\ref{Metric.IsCoarselyGeodesic}) and for all $c, b \ge 0$ there is $\delta$ such
  that every $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic triangle in $X$ is $\delta$-slim.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Differences from L\"oh's definition.} L\"oh asks that $X$ be $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic
for some $c, b > 0$, and that for all $c' \ge c$, $b' \ge b$ there be $\delta$ such that the
$(c', b')$-quasi-geodesic triangles are $\delta$-slim. Here $X$ is coarsely geodesic instead, a
chain condition equivalent to L\"oh's with other constants
(Theorem~\ref{Metric.isCoarselyGeodesic_iff_isQuasiGeodesicSpace}), and the triangles are
slim for all $c, b \ge 0$. Nothing is lost: a $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic is a
$(c', b')$-quasi-geodesic whenever $c \le c'$ and $b \le b'$, so the
triangles for small constants are among those for large ones. Without a hypothesis on $X$ the
condition would hold in spaces where quasi-geodesics join only nearby points, such as
$\{2^n \mid n \in \N\} \subseteq \R$. As for slim geodesic triangles, $\delta$-slimness is asked of
every side, which is the same as asking it of the first side of every triangle.

\begin{lemma}[Invariance of quasi-hyperbolicity, L\"oh Prop.~7.2.9]
  \label{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}
  \uses{Metric.IsQuasiHyperbolic, Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding, Metric.IsCoarselyGeodesic}
  Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a quasi-isometric embedding of pseudometric spaces. If $X$ is coarsely
  geodesic and $Y$ is quasi-hyperbolic, then $X$ is quasi-hyperbolic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{Metric.IsQuasiGeodesicWith}
  Let $f$ be a $(c_0, b_0)$-quasi-isometric embedding and $c, b \ge 0$. Composed with $f$, a
  $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic of $X$ is a $(c', b')$-quasi-geodesic of $Y$, with $c', b'$ depending
  only on $c, b, c_0, b_0$; take $\delta$ for $c', b'$ in $Y$. The image under $f$ of a
  $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic triangle of $X$ is a $(c', b')$-quasi-geodesic triangle of $Y$, so for a
  point $p$ of one side there is a point $q$ of another side with $d(f p, f q) \le \delta$. The
  lower bound of $f$ gives $d(p, q) \le c_0 \delta + c_0 b_0$. So the $(c, b)$-quasi-geodesic
  triangles of $X$ are $c_0(\delta + b_0)$-slim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolicity and quasi-hyperbolicity, L\"oh Thm.~7.2.10]
  \label{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith}
  \uses{Metric.IsQuasiHyperbolic, Metric.HasSlimTrianglesWith, Metric.IsGeodesic}
  A geodesic pseudometric space is quasi-hyperbolic if and only if it has $\delta$-slim triangles
  for some $\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{Metric.HasSlimTrianglesWith.exists_hausdorffDist_le,
    Metric.IsGeodesicSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith, Metric.IsQuasiGeodesicWith}
  ``Only if'': a geodesic triangle is a $(1, 0)$-quasi-geodesic triangle, so take $\delta$ for $c =
  1$, $b = 0$. ``If'': a geodesic space is coarsely geodesic
  (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsGeodesicSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}). Let the triangles be
  $\delta$-slim, $c, b \ge 0$, and $R$ as in
  Theorem~\ref{Metric.HasSlimTrianglesWith.exists_hausdorffDist_le}. Given a $(c,
  b)$-quasi-geodesic triangle, choose geodesics between its vertices; they form a $\delta$-slim
  geodesic triangle. A point of one quasi-geodesic side is within $R + 1$ of a point of the
  geodesic side with the same endpoints, which is within $\delta$ of a point of another geodesic
  side, which is within $R + 1$ of a point of the corresponding quasi-geodesic side. So the
  quasi-geodesic triangle is $(2R + \delta + 2)$-slim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Hyperbolicity and quasi-isometric embeddings, Bridson--Haefliger Thm.~III.H.1.9,
  L\"oh Cor.~7.2.13]
  \label{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith}
  \uses{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding, Metric.HasSlimTrianglesWith, Metric.IsGeodesic}
  Let $X$ and $Y$ be geodesic pseudometric spaces and $f \colon X \to Y$ a quasi-isometric
  embedding. If $Y$ has $\delta$-slim triangles for some $\delta$, then $X$ has $\delta'$-slim
  triangles for some $\delta'$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith,
    Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic,
    Metric.IsGeodesicSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}
  $Y$ is quasi-hyperbolic (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith}),
  $X$ is coarsely geodesic (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsGeodesicSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}), so $X$ is
  quasi-hyperbolic (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}) and has slim
  triangles (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith} again).
\end{proof}

This is L\"oh's proof. Bridson--Haefliger prove the corollary directly from the stability of
quasi-geodesics, without quasi-hyperbolicity.

\section*{Hyperbolic groups}

\inputleannode{Group.Generators.IsHyperbolicWith}

L\"oh (Definition 7.3.1) calls a finitely generated group hyperbolic if its Cayley graph for some,
hence every, finite generating set is quasi-hyperbolic. The Cayley graph with its graph metric is
the group with its word metric
(Theorem~\ref{Group.Generators.dist_cayleySimpleGraph}), and the word metric of a generating
family is that of the set of its elements. ``Hence every'' is elementary for quasi-hyperbolicity
(Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic} and the change of generators,
Corollary~\ref{Group.Generators.WithWordMetric.isQuasiIsometry_congr}), but not for the
four-point condition. The definition above agrees with L\"oh's
(Theorem~\ref{Group.Generators.isHyperbolic_iff_isQuasiHyperbolic}), through the following
theorem, which needs the stability of quasi-geodesics.

\begin{theorem}[Four-point condition and quasi-hyperbolicity for word metrics]
  \label{Group.Generators.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff}
  \uses{Metric.IsQuasiHyperbolic, Group.Generators.IsHyperbolicWith,
    Group.Generators.WithWordMetric}
  Let $P$ be a finite generating family of a group $G$. The word metric of $P$ is quasi-hyperbolic
  if and only if it satisfies the four-point condition with some constant $\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{SimpleGraph.Realization, SimpleGraph.Realization.dist_vertex,
    SimpleGraph.Realization.isGeodesicSpace, Group.Generators.dist_cayleySimpleGraph,
    Metric.isQuasiIsometry_iff_quasiDenseRange, Group.Generators.isCoarselyGeodesicWith,
    Metric.IsHyperbolicWith.of_isometry_of_quasiDenseRange,
    Metric.IsHyperbolicWith.hasSlimTrianglesWith, Metric.HasSlimTrianglesWith.isHyperbolicWith,
    Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith,
    Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}
  The Cayley graph of $P$ is connected, as $P$ generates $G$; write $|P|$ for its geometric
  realisation (Definition~\ref{SimpleGraph.Realization}) and $\iota \colon (G, d_P) \to |P|$ for the
  vertex map. By Lemma~\ref{SimpleGraph.Realization.dist_vertex} and
  Theorem~\ref{Group.Generators.dist_cayleySimpleGraph}, $\iota$ is isometric and every point of
  $|P|$ is within $\frac12$ of its image, so it is a quasi-isometry
  (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isQuasiIsometry_iff_quasiDenseRange}); $|P|$ is geodesic
  (Lemma~\ref{SimpleGraph.Realization.isGeodesicSpace}) and $(G, d_P)$ is coarsely geodesic
  (Lemma~\ref{Group.Generators.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}).

  ``If'': by Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsHyperbolicWith.of_isometry_of_quasiDenseRange}, $|P|$ satisfies
  the four-point condition with constant $\delta + 3$, so it has slim triangles
  (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsHyperbolicWith.hasSlimTrianglesWith}) and is quasi-hyperbolic
  (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith}); then so is $(G, d_P)$,
  through $\iota$ (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}).

  ``Only if'': a quasi-inverse of $\iota$ is a quasi-isometric embedding $|P| \to (G, d_P)$, and
  $|P|$ is coarsely geodesic, so $|P|$ is quasi-hyperbolic
  (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}), has slim triangles
  (Theorem~\ref{Metric.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff_exists_hasSlimTrianglesWith}) and satisfies the
  four-point condition (Lemma~\ref{Metric.HasSlimTrianglesWith.isHyperbolicWith}). It passes to the
  isometric image of $(G, d_P)$ with the same constant (restriction to a subspace, in the copied
  pull request).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic groups, cf.\ L\"oh Def.~7.3.1]
  \label{Group.Generators.isHyperbolic_iff_isQuasiHyperbolic}
  \uses{Group.Generators.IsHyperbolicWith, Metric.IsQuasiHyperbolic,
    Group.Generators.WithWordMetric}
  Let $P$ be a finite generating family of a group $G$. Then $G$ is hyperbolic if and only if the
  word metric of $P$ is quasi-hyperbolic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{Group.Generators.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff,
    Group.Generators.WithWordMetric.isQuasiIsometry_congr,
    Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic, Group.Generators.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}
  ``Only if'': some finite generating family $Q$ has a word metric satisfying the four-point
  condition, which is then quasi-hyperbolic
  (Theorem~\ref{Group.Generators.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff}). The identity $(G, d_P) \to (G, d_Q)$ is a
  quasi-isometry (Corollary~\ref{Group.Generators.WithWordMetric.isQuasiIsometry_congr}) and $(G,
  d_P)$ is coarsely geodesic (Lemma~\ref{Group.Generators.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}), so $(G, d_P)$
  is quasi-hyperbolic (Lemma~\ref{Metric.IsQuasiIsometricEmbedding.isQuasiHyperbolic}). ``If'': by
  Theorem~\ref{Group.Generators.isQuasiHyperbolic_iff} the word metric of $P$ satisfies the
  four-point condition. Reindexing $P$ along a bijection from $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ to its index
  set does not change the word metric, so $G$ is hyperbolic.
\end{proof}
