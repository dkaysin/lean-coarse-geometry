\chapter{Coarsely geodesic spaces}
\label{chap:coarsely-geodesic}

Milestone M3. An \emph{$r$-chain} from $x$ to $y$ is a sequence $x = x_0, \dots, x_n = y$ with
$d(x_i, x_{i+1}) \le r$; $n$ is its number of steps. In Lean a chain is a walk in the Rips graph at
scale $r$, so that $n$ is the length of the walk (design decisions D6, D13). The definitions follow
Cornulier--de la Harpe, Def.~3.B.1 (a), (c) and Def.~3.B.5; their name for our coarsely geodesic
spaces is \emph{large-scale geodesic}.

\paragraph{Why chains.} The chain hypothesis replaces quasi-geodesic paths in the \v{S}varc--Milnor
lemma (Chapter~\ref{chap:svarc-milnor}). It makes the proof a finite counting argument, needs no
theory of geodesic spaces, and holds for word metrics themselves ($r = L = 1$, $C = 0$), so the
corollaries follow by letting a group act on itself. No generality is lost: in every pseudometric
space the chain condition is equivalent, with other constants, to L\"oh's (non-continuous)
quasi-geodesic condition (Definition 5.3.4).

\begin{lemma}[Geodesic spaces are coarsely geodesic]
  \label{Metric.IsGeodesicSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith}
  \uses{Metric.IsGeodesic, Metric.IsCoarselyGeodesicWith}
  A geodesic pseudometric space is $(r, 1/r, 1)$-coarsely geodesic for every $r > 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{Metric.exists_ripsGraph_walk_length_le}
  Let $\gamma$ be a geodesic from $x$ to $y$, $d = d(x, y)$ and $m = \max\{\lceil d/r \rceil,
  1\}$, so that $m \le d/r + 1$. The points $\gamma(kd/m)$, $k = 0, \dots, m$, are consecutively
  at distance $d/m \le r$, so Lemma~\ref{Metric.exists_ripsGraph_walk_length_le} gives a walk from
  $x$ to $y$ in the Rips graph at scale $r$ with at most $m$ steps. This is the proof of
  Lemma~\ref{NormedSpace.isCoarselyGeodesicWith} with the geodesic in place of the segment.
\end{proof}
